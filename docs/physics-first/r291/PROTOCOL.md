# Measurement definitions before reanalysis

Use the meridional area element dr dz and oriented normal vorticity w. For an isolated ring the signed flux of the full section is Γ. Let r25, r50 and r75 be the first radii about the spline ridge enclosing respectively 0.25, 0.50 and 0.75 times the **same release Γ**. A radius is valid only where the cumulative signed flux has a unique crossing around the selected core. Check against the quadratic peak, circulation centroid and carried geometric-line marker. A radius can grow when a section flattens even at fixed area.

Define effective area as (∫w)² / ∫w², without the one-half enstrophy convention. It equals the area of a uniform patch and 2πa² for a planar Gaussian. Full-section signed integrals remove the need to pick a core edge. Measure the positive-weight covariance widths √(2λ) and π times their product on the full section as well. Expand finite disks independently and record the circulation omitted. Mixed signs or an assigned flux near zero invalidate a parent interpretation. Compute each release-relative change using the identical observable at both times. Check field resolution, polar integration order, ridge estimate and sampling independently. Compare with the saved viscous and inviscid histories and the same Gaussian ring broadened by pure heat flow.

For a material azimuthal segment, length is proportional to its radius r and its rate is uᵣ/r. Compare intensity on the same fluid with stretching and viscous terms. Close the moving-disk circulation change against advection, viscosity and rim velocity. Require declining fixed-fraction radii and effective area, consistent widths, retained circulation, material elongation and intensification before inferring stretching-driven contraction. Report shape distortion or slower spreading separately. Preserve the historical quarter-signal uncertainty test over three consecutive outputs. Compare like-aged pair and isolated measurements. End individual-parent attribution when contours cease separating the parents.

Distinguish an error in a flux derivative from attribution of historical nonlinear divergence.
