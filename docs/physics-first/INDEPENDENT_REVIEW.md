## The physical question

Can a finite vortex in ordinary water narrow through its own motion, or through measurable work at a wall or port? At what radius does stretching lose to diffusion, instability, cavitation, compressibility or another effect? We require conservation of mass, momentum and total energy, a liquid equation of state (EOS), viscous stress and heat conduction. For unforced evolution the energy comes from the initial state. For boundary-driven evolution we must account for the force, torque and work at the boundary. Artificial volume forces and prescribed collapsing profiles are outside both cases.

We start at 20°C and 101325 Pa absolute. The radius of **peak swirl speed** is 10 mm and that speed is 0.1 m/s. With ν ≈ 10⁻⁶ m²/s, density ≈ 998 kg/m³ and sound speed ≈ 1482 m/s, the Reynolds number based on that radius is 1000 and the Mach number is 6.75×10⁻⁵. Sound crosses the core in 6.75 μs. Swirl advects across a core radius in 0.1 s. This time separation makes a pressure pulse easy to measure and a slowly spreading vortex easy to overdamp numerically. ## What a fresh reading changes

**Unforced mathematical result to watch.** On 20 November, Alpöge and Buckmaster posted a claim of finite-time breakdown from smooth initial data for the three-dimensional *incompressible*, viscous, unforced equations in whole space and on the torus. Their proposed flow approaches a profile repeating at discrete changes of scale, up to a spatial isometry. A central step requires a numerical certificate. In the authors’ dated release the Lean development covers implications of its inequalities, while the certificate and its interval-arithmetic verification are absent. We read the manuscript and release notes and have not independently verified the claim. If the result holds, specially prepared initial motion alone can produce extreme gradients in that model. It gives no water EOS, laboratory preparation or core radius in meters. A transferable idea for eventual 3D data is to compare entire recentered velocity and vorticity fields across scales, allowing rotation, and test repeatability under refinement. A fitted peak alone cannot establish that mechanism. [20 November manuscript](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/paper/finite-time-blow-up-navier-stokes.pdf) · [Authors’ description of verification](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/README.md)

**Why the September result has different experimental implications.** OpenAI’s construction starts from rest with a specially designed smooth volume force. Lei and Ren’s November exposition explains how oscillatory corrections carry momentum through their averaged quadratic flux. Clay’s announcement describes an ongoing evaluation. For a finite tank, the question worth borrowing is where angular momentum enters the narrowing region. Measure its flux through an inner cylinder and compare with separately measured wall torque. An older rigorous compressible example, Merle et al.’s implosion for particular barotropic pressure laws, instead concentrates *density*. Recording both density and material stretching distinguishes two processes that a shrinking length scale alone confounds. [OpenAI construction](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/) · [Lei and Ren, Part II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06869) · [Clay announcement](https://www.claymath.org/news/navier-stokes-announcement/) · [Merle et al., Annals of Mathematics](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2022/196-2/p04)

**A low-Mach vortex is a separate numerical test.** Orlando and Bonaventura evolved a Gresho vortex with a stiffened-gas EOS parametrized for water and tracked kinetic energy over three rotations. This is an inviscid rotating benchmark using their time integrator. Its lesson for SU2 is to test the discrete centrifugal–pressure balance and the decay of a rotational mode. An acoustic pass can coexist with excess damping of that mode. Keep the liquid EOS and true sound speed for our physical run. [Orlando and Bonaventura, §5.5 and Table 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09252)

**A finite water experiment already exhibits a mechanism we can observe.** Knister, Ganesh and Ceccio generated two unequal counter-rotating vortices behind hydrofoils. Instability stretched secondary vortices. The researchers combined core pressures *inferred* from velocity measurements with measured nuclei populations to predict inception rates, then compared them with directly observed cavitation events. A tracked secondary core can narrow even as the larger structure loses coherence. In a later 3D test, track each core, record its radius and circulation in its local normal plane, and record the pressure history along its path. Vapor pressure is a useful single-phase stop criterion; predicting bubble inception also takes nuclei and exposure history. The hydrofoil experiment is a candidate comparison if the reviewer prioritizes observed concentration. [Vortex measurements](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2026.11120) · [Cavitation measurements](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2026.11441)

Two other ideas belong on the later 3D list. Schorlepp, Rosenhaus and Falkovich obtained colliding vorticity waves along a tube in an optimized, randomly forced incompressible model. This suggests looking for axial propagation and convergence of vorticity peaks separately from sound. Duraiswami’s porous-wall study, already in the historical plan, supplies a boundary-driven comparison with specified inflow and swirl. For either mechanism, test how much interior strain is actually produced for the available energy and whether coherence survives. These studies do not set a threshold for our water. [Vorticity-wave preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13056) · [Porous-wall preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17642)

## Which small experiment should come next?

**Our first choice is the water vortex at time zero.** Use the saved smooth swirl, tapered to zero between 60 and 80 mm, a cylinder radius of 100 mm, an axial period of 100 mm, zero radial/axial velocity, uniform 293.15 K and 101325 Pa at the initial wall. Integrate dp/dr = ρ(p,T)uθ²/r inward on 16, 32 and 64 radial cells per initial peak radius. The regular axis has a vanishing centrifugal term. Store density, momentum and total-energy density, recover p and T through the solver’s thermodynamic variables, and compare two independent measures:

1. For the integrated pressure, compare p(30 mm)−p(0) with 15.902 Pa, accounting for the reference density of 998 kg/m³ versus the liquid EOS value of 998.207 kg/m³. An independent fixed-temperature check is the integral of dp/ρ(p,T) between the endpoint pressures against the integral of uθ²/r in radius.
2. For local balance, evaluate the recovered radial pressure gradient minus ρuθ²/r in Pa/m. Report its peak, location, radial norm and refinement trend. A small integrated error can hide a large local mismatch near the axis or taper. State whether the residual comes from an independent radial stencil or SU2’s actual momentum operator. Only the latter measures the solver’s initial radial acceleration after division by density.

A proposed acceptance scale is 0.05 Pa on the inner pressure rise, inherited from the spreading benchmark. For the reviewer to challenge, 1% of the initial peak-speed centrifugal scale ρU²/R is about 10 Pa/m, or 0.01 m/s² of spurious radial acceleration. Specify the norm and tolerance before execution, requiring improvement under refinement as well as a small finest-grid residual. If the pressure round trip or the balance fails to converge, stop at initialization and diagnose the discrepancy. Success would establish consistent initial data. Preserving those data over a sound crossing is a subsequent test.

**A credible alternative is a transverse shear wave.** In a periodic box impose a small uᵧ varying sinusoidally along x, with uniform initial pressure, density and temperature. At linear order its amplitude decays as exp(−νk²t), with k the wavenumber. The inferred decay rate directly measures effective viscosity, a quantity the longitudinal wave left unconstrained. The reviewer could put this first if excess numerical diffusion is the larger risk, choosing a wavelength and duration that resolve decay cheaply. A defensible gate would compare the inferred viscosity across grids and time steps, with the uncertainty below the physical damping one hopes to measure. Neither option requires a new solver, long FEM run or 3D DNS.
