# First time-dependent water-ring experiment

**Decision A.** Test the 8 mm gap, 15 mm ring radius, 2 mm Gaussian width, 60° included angle and Γ = 0.0036 m²/s per ring against a matched isolated ring. Begin with the first **20 ms** of free evolution. The candidate’s facing peak has an initial material tendency of +0.142 s⁻¹, and two strong radial shoulders gain vorticity across every one-factor preparation trial. The experiment asks whether a material segment lengthens and its sectional area falls measurably below release, and whether viscosity, tilting or flattening instead accounts for the observations. The most informative negative outcome would be growth of peak vorticity alongside increasing area. [Preparation and uncertainty](RING_PREPARATION.md) · [Sectional evidence](RING_SECTIONS.md)

## Qualification before a pair run

The installed SU2 8.6.0 / CoolProp 7.2.0 reproduced small-amplitude acoustic propagation at the physical sound speed. Removing fourth-order JST dissipation brought the 64-cell shear viscosity within 0.081% of material ν and the fixed-step refinement approached second order. At 128 cells and acoustic CFL 0.50 the cross-strip pressure range reached 0.07090 Pa against a 0.001 Pa limit. At CFL 0.25 it returned below 0.00060 Pa. The rotating initial-state check reproduced a 15.9056 Pa pressure rise but its finest recovered-pressure acceleration failed the refinement test. That measurement used a post-processing stencil. The actual rotating discrete momentum residual and slow-time stability remain open. If the short window under-resolves decay, label that metric unresolved and measure it in the qualified slow-time window.

Repeat the shear phase/step and acoustic wave controls for any changed operator. Require the historical 0.001 Pa cross-strip gates at both phases, <1% error in resolved acoustic phase speed and amplitude, and <0.02 s⁻¹ difference in measured rotating vorticity rate. Lengthen the 2D rotating control to 20 ms with independent physical-step halving and tighter inner solves. Recheck against the viscous reference and the budgets below. A short acoustic pass alone does not qualify slow-time evolution.
3. Run the **3D isolated ring first** with the candidate parameters, at the left ring's position and tilt and with the same domain and discretization as the pair. ## Initial fields and thermodynamics

With σ = −1,+1, Cσ = (σ16.99038,0,0) mm, eσ = (cos 30°,0,σ sin 30°), define Xσ(θ) = Cσ + R(eσ cosθ + eᵧ sinθ), oriented by increasing θ. In free space set Gₐ(x) = exp(−|x|²/a²)/(π³ᐟ²a³), ω = ΓΣ∮Gₐ(x−Xσ)dXσ, and u = Γ/(4π)Σ∮[dXσ×(x−Xσ)]K, with K the enclosed Gaussian mass divided by distance cubed. Its limit at coincidence is finite. Closed loops give solenoidal ω and dipolar far velocity O(r⁻³), hence finite kinetic energy. Omit the partner for the control. The quadrature gives a free-space pair energy of 0.375 mJ. [Kernel and derivatives](RING_STRAIN.md) · [Energy integral](RING_PREPARATION.md)

For the periodic box, periodize ω and use the compatible discrete curl inverse, e.g. û(k) = i k×ω̂(k)/|k|² for nonzero Fourier modes on a uniform reference grid, with û(0)=0. Each closed curve has zero integrated vorticity. Use a discrete Helmholtz projection or equivalent face-flux construction on the evolution mesh. Check div u, curl u and reconstructed sections against the periodic reference after averaging to control volumes. Direct truncation of the free-space velocity at the periodic seam would create a spurious layer.

Use p = 101325 Pa + p′, T = 293.15 K initially. Solve the periodic low-Mach pressure balance Δp′ = −ρ₀ ∂ᵢuⱼ∂ⱼuᵢ on the **combined** field, with zero mean p′. Check its source mean before removing discrete roundoff, the Poisson residual, and the irrotational part of the actual discrete momentum acceleration. Vortical accelerations are physical. Set ρ and e by a liquid-branch (p,T) flash, then store (ρ,ρu,ρ[e+|u|²/2]). Recover (p,T,c) from the stored conservative state on a fresh EOS path. Use measured water transport including shear viscosity and heat conduction, with ζ=0 as the project's slow-swirl bulk-viscosity approximation. Evaluate sensitivity to ζ if dilatation affects the budget. Evolve total energy and the full thermodynamic pressure at the true sound speed. Check phase and p_abs−p_sat(T) throughout. The initial solenoidal velocity and Poisson pressure are well-prepared to leading order in Mach number. Startup sound, dilatation, variable viscosity and baroclinic torque are measurable residual effects. Set all forcing, moving boundaries and heat sources to zero. ## Domain, grid and resource estimate

Use a 160 mm periodic cube for the preliminary test, centered on the pair, with a 320 mm comparison at the same near-core spacing. This reduces the original design's production box only if its image error passes. Before evolving, compare periodic and free-space u, ∇u, full projected net, and section circulation on both rings. Require |Δu| < 1% of isolated speed, |Δnet| < 0.01 s⁻¹ and circulation change <0.2% Γ. Repeat the short pair and isolated measurements in the larger box. Require the change in the pair-minus-single width signal to be <¼ of the claimed change; otherwise enlarge again or report it unresolved. The physical tank has no-slip walls, hydrostatic head and acoustically reflecting boundaries. The periodic model is an idealization of the interior release.

The nested estimate uses an 80×48×48 mm fine region around both rings within a 112×80×80 mm middle region. Subtract the cells replaced at each level. Allocate adequate fine padding for motion (isolated flight ~1.3 mm in 20 ms), then adapt to the actual smallest principal width and inter-core layer. Restriction, prolongation, pressure solve and refluxing must conserve mass, momentum and energy. The memory range is a planning assumption for conservative fields, stages, gradients, EOS and pressure work arrays. Unstructured connectivity, ghost cells, multigrid/Krylov vectors, allocator overhead and outputs can raise the peak. Daisy reports 7.6 GiB total and about 5 GiB available during this phase. Even the nested layout needs a measured bytes/cell and wall-time pilot before scheduling, with at least 25% RAM headroom and no swap dependence. The full uniform fine runs need a larger host. Keep the 2D qualification local. Use three near-core spacings, e.g. 0.5, ⅓ and 0.25 mm, with an extra finer check if the claimed difference is unresolved at 8 cells/a; the 4-cell/a case is diagnostic.

At Δx = 0.25 mm and c = 1482 m/s, an explicit Cartesian step with summed acoustic CFL 0.2 is ~11 ns, or 1.8 million steps for 20 ms. A sharper unstructured cell can be stricter. For a candidate acoustic-implicit integrator try physical steps of 100, 50 and 25 µs (200, 400 and 800 steps). At velocity scale 0.25 m/s, 50 µs gives one-direction advective CFL 0.05 and νΔt/Δx² ≈ 0.00080. Core turnover is ~77 ms using peak swirl at 1.121a. The 160 mm box sound crossing is 108 µs. Slow-time steps under-resolve some acoustic periods, so compare short explicit segments, record startup probes at acoustic cadence and independently halve the physical step. Stability alone cannot establish pressure or vorticity accuracy.

## Method decision

- The installed conservative density-based explicit SU2/CoolProp route has physical acoustics, viscous terms and an EOS path with archived validation. Use it for short reference windows. Small pressure changes and mesh-dependent artificial dissipation have already limited rotating/shear accuracy.
- A conservative pressure-based all-Mach finite-volume method with implicit acoustic fluxes and explicit advection/physical viscosity is a suitable slow-time candidate. A general liquid EOS requires a consistent pressure/energy solve and thermodynamic derivatives. An implicit dual-time method on the installed solver is another candidate if its liquid-EOS path actually supports time-accurate physical stepping. Verify conservation, order, low-Mach dissipation and convergence as inner tolerance is tightened by 10×. Availability and performance are unqualified. Preserve the true physical sound speed during physical time advancement. [Boscheri and Pareschi, general-EOS IMEX formulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01789)
- A divergence-free incompressible viscous solution can be an independent velocity/width control at this Mach number. It removes acoustic and thermodynamic observables. A regularized filament model can estimate path and timing before overlap; its prescribed width supplies no test of narrowing. Neither qualifies the compressible-water experiment.

## Measurements and prospective decision limits

Log the pair and isolated controls at release and at least every 0.5 ms. Add high-cadence material probes over the first accepted timesteps for a t→0 extrapolation. Use advected markers initially placed on each centerline, alongside a separately identified vorticity ridge. Viscous diffusion permits the ridge and material markers to separate. Measure material segment length and total ridge length separately. Reslice normal to the local ridge, track the vorticity direction, and measure at facing, both shoulders, flanks and far arcs. Recompute the ridge, tangent and aperture under refinement.
